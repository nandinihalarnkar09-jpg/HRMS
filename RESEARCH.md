## Part A — Research questions

### Q1. What details does an Indian HR team keep for every employee?

For Konkan, PAN, Aadhaar, UAN, ESIC number and bank details are all effectively mandatory, and every field group needs a rule for who can see it.

**Why all of them apply to Konkan.** Konkan has 180 employees. The provident fund chapter of the Code on Social Security applies to every establishment with 20 or more employees (Taxmann). The PF wage ceiling rose from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month from 17 September 2026, so more staff are now mandatory members (AZB & Partners). ESIC covers employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month (₹25,000 for persons with disability), which will include many plant workers (Vikaspedia). Konkan's HRMS only stores these numbers; payroll computes the contributions.

| Field group | Fields | Who can see it |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | Full name as on PAN/Aadhaar, date of birth, gender, photo | Name and photo: everyone. DOB and gender: self and HR |
| Contact | Mobile, personal and work email, current and permanent address | Self (edits own), HR. Manager: work email and mobile of direct reports |
| Emergency contact | Name, relation, phone | Self (edits own), HR |
| Job | Employee code, date of joining, designation, department, location, reporting manager, employment type, status | Self (read-only), manager (direct reports), HR (edits) |
| Salary grade | Grade or band | Self and HR only (proposal: managers don't need it for attendance or leave) |
| Statutory | PAN, Aadhaar, UAN, ESIC number | Self: masked. HR: masked by default, full value only through a logged Reveal. Manager: never |
| Bank | Account number, IFSC, bank name | Self: last 4 digits. HR: masked, logged Reveal. Manager: never |
| Attendance and leave | Punches, day status, balances, requests | Self, manager (direct reports), HR |
| Medical certificates | SL certificate uploads | Self, the approving manager for that request, HR |
| Audit log | Who changed what, old and new values | HR only |
| Exit | Last working day, reason | HR; manager sees last working day of direct reports |

The AI agent never receives statutory or bank fields, even masked (brief guardrail).

**Mandatory in practice**
- **PAN:** needed for TDS on salary. Without it, tax is deducted at the higher of the rate in force or 20% (Income Tax Department). From 1 April 2026 this rule moved from section 206AA to section 397(2) of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (TDSMAN).
- **UAN:** required because PF applies to Konkan. The brief makes it optional for new joiners, who may not have one yet.
- **Aadhaar:** effectively required because EPFO needs it linked to the UAN. After the 2018 Puttaswamy judgment, a private employer can't demand it for unrelated purposes.
- **ESIC number:** for employees within the ₹21,000 ceiling.
- **Bank details:** needed for the payroll vendor to credit salary.

**Sensitive fields:** Aadhaar (store it, but never display it in full), PAN, bank details, salary grade, medical certificates, and disciplinary or exit reasons. The old IT Act SPDI Rules (2011) listed financial and health data as sensitive. The DPDP Act protects all personal data without a separate sensitive category.

**DPDP timeline:** the government notified the DPDP Rules on 13 November 2025. Notice, consent, breach-reporting and security duties start on 12 May 2027 (AZB & Partners). Employment is a legitimate use, so HR mostly doesn't need consent, but safeguards still apply (Tsaaro). A system Konkan builds now should already meet those duties.

### Q2. How is an employee code formed, and why should it never change?

Konkan's code is KCF plus a 4-digit zero-padded running number (KCF0001, KCF0002 …), generated by the system and never changed or reused.

**Format rule.** Prefix KCF, then the next number from a database sequence, padded to 4 digits. Four digits allow 9,999 employees, far beyond Konkan's 180. HR never types the code.

**Keep it meaningless.** Don't add location, department or grade, such as KCF-VRN-0042 for Verna. An employee who moves from the Verna plant to the Panaji head office would then carry a wrong code. Never reuse the code of someone who has left.

**Why it never changes.** The code is the key that ties together:
- payroll history and Form 16
- PF and ESI filings
- attendance and leave balances
- the month-end summary sent to the payroll vendor
- audit trails and legal record retention after exit

If it changes, history splits into two records, reports break and statutory filings stop matching. A rehired employee gets their old code back.

### Q3. Leave types in Indian companies; statutory vs company policy

Most companies offer CL, SL and EL plus maternity, paternity, comp-off and LOP; only maternity and a minimum of earned, casual and sick leave are set by law.

| Leave | Typical practice | Konkan policy |
|---|---|---|
| CL (casual) | 6–8 days for short, unplanned needs; lapses at year end | 8 days, lapses 31 Dec, half day allowed |
| SL (sick) | 6–12 days; certificate often needed after 2–3 days | 7 days, lapses 31 Dec, certificate if more than 2 consecutive days |
| EL/PL (earned/privilege) | 12–21+ days for planned leave; accrues, carries forward, encashable | 15 days at 1.25 per completed month, carry forward up to 30, no half day |
| Maternity | Statutory, see below | As per law |
| Paternity | No statutory right in the private sector; companies give 3–15 days | 5 days (company policy) |
| LOP (loss of pay) | Unpaid absence deducted from salary | Unlimited, half day allowed |
| Comp-off | For working on a holiday or weekly off; expires in 30–90 days | Not in Konkan's policy |

**Maternity (Code on Social Security, 2020, section 60).** The Code replaced the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 when the labour codes took effect on 21 November 2025 (iPleaders).
- **Eligibility:** the woman must have actually worked at least 80 days in the 12 months before her expected delivery date (PIB).
- **Fewer than two surviving children:** 26 weeks, of which no more than 8 before delivery (Taxmann). "Fewer than two surviving children" is the legal test, not "first two births".
- **Two or more surviving children:** 12 weeks, of which no more than 6 before delivery.
- **Adoption or commissioning (surrogacy) mother:** 12 weeks from the date the child is handed over. The Code originally limited adoption leave to a child under 3 months, but on 17 March 2026 the Supreme Court struck down that age limit, so any legal adoption now qualifies (India Briefing). Older notes and reviews that still apply the 3-month rule are out of date.

**Paternity.** There is still no statutory paternity leave in the private sector. In the same March 2026 ruling the Supreme Court asked the Central Government to legislate it (iPleaders). Konkan's 5 days is pure company policy.

**Statutory entitlement** is the legal minimum an employer can't go below; breaching it attracts penalties. **Company policy** is whatever the employer gives on top, set in the contract or handbook, and may be more generous. Goa's Shops Act says any more favourable right from a contract or custom continues (Goa Act 23 of 2025, s.38).

A Konkan example: its 15 days of EL matches the Goa Act's 15 days, but its 7 days of SL is below the Goa Act's 9 days for covered workers (see Q5 and open questions). In the HRMS, each rule should be marked as a statutory floor or a policy choice.

### Q4. How does leave accrue, and what happens at year end?

Leave is credited up front, monthly or per days worked, pro-rated for joiners; at year end it lapses, carries forward to a cap, or is encashed. Each method below has a real example.

| Method | Real example | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Front-loaded | Central government staff get 15 days of EL credited in advance on 1 January and again on 1 July | CCS (Leave) Rules, Rule 26 |
| Monthly | Adobe India gives 1.25 vacation days a month, 15 a year | Adobe India benefits |
| Pro-rated for joiners | A central government joiner gets 2.5 days of EL per completed calendar month in the half-year they join | CCS (Leave) Rules, Rule 27 |
| Per days worked (statutory) | Plant workers earn 1 day per 20 days worked once they work 180+ days in a calendar year | OSH Code s.32, via Simpliance |
| Lapse | Under Goa's Shops Act, unused sick and casual leave lapse at year end | Goa Act 23 of 2025, s.20(2) |
| Carry forward | Adobe India carries unused vacation up to 45 days; central government EL up to 300 days | Adobe, CCS Rules |
| Encashment | Under Goa's Shops Act, a worker refused leave can encash EL above 45 days; the OSH Code allows yearly encashment above its 30-day cap | Goa Act s.20(5), Simpliance |

**Statutory figures.** Under the OSH Code (in force 21 November 2025), a worker qualifies for annual leave after 180 days of work in a calendar year, down from 240, and earns 1 day per 20 days worked (factoHR). A mid-year joiner qualifies if they work one-fourth of the remaining days of that year (Simpliance). Carry-forward is capped at 30 days, with the excess encashable (Simpliance). On exit, all accrued leave must be paid (Acuity Law).

**Tax.** Leave encashed during service is taxable. Leave encashed on retirement or resignation is exempt up to ₹25 lakh for non-government employees, raised from ₹3 lakh by CBDT Notification 31/2023 from 1 April 2023 (CBDT press release via GConnect).

**The pro-rating problem.** "Joining in June gets 10.5 days" only works for someone joining on 1 June. Take Konkan's 8 CL for a 15 June joiner:
- counting completed months only (July–December = 6): 8 × 6 ÷ 12 = 4.0
- counting June as a full month (7 months): 8 × 7 ÷ 12 = 4.67
- counting June as half a month (6.5 months): 8 × 6.5 ÷ 12 = 4.33

Each result then needs a rounding rule: whole or half days, up, down or nearest. The central government rounds fractions to the nearest whole day (CCS Rules summary, ISTM). Konkan's own test case is easier: a 1 July joiner gets CL 8 × 6 ÷ 12 = 4 and SL 7 × 6 ÷ 12 = 3.5 under any method. My proposed answer is rule L4 below; confirm it before building the balance screen.

**Caveat.** Several states are still notifying their labour-code rules, and how the OSH Code fits with state Shops Acts is not yet settled (Bar & Bench).

### Q5. Shops and Establishments Act — Goa

Konkan's Panaji head office falls under Goa's new Shops Act, which caps work at 48 hours a week and gives 6 CL, 9 SL, 15 EL and 9 paid holidays; the Verna plant is a factory and falls under the OSH Code instead.

**The Act.** The Goa Shops and Establishments (Regulation of Employment and Conditions of Service) Act, 2025 (Goa Act 23 of 2025) was assented to on 19 September 2025 and published in the Gazette on 1 October 2025. It replaces the Goa, Daman and Diu Shops and Establishments Act, 1973, and starts on a date the state government notifies (Goa Act 23 of 2025, via PRS).

**Who it covers.** Its full provisions apply to establishments with 20 or more workers; smaller ones only send an online intimation (s.1(3), s.7). It excludes workers in confidential, managerial or supervisory roles and workers whose average monthly wages exceed ₹24,000 (s.3). Factories are excluded: a "shop" and a "commercial establishment" both exclude a factory (s.2).

| Area | Goa Act, 2025 |
|---|---|
| Daily and weekly hours | Max 10 hours a day and 48 a week (s.12) |
| Rest break | At least 30 minutes after 5 hours of continuous work (s.12) |
| Spread-over | Max 12 hours a day, 14 for intermittent or urgent work (s.13) |
| Overtime | Beyond 8 hours a day or 48 a week, at twice the ordinary wage; max 144 hours in 3 months (s.14) |
| Weekly off | Open all 7 days allowed, but each worker gets 24 consecutive hours of rest a week; a denied weekly off is given back within 2 months (s.15) |
| Work on weekly off | Twice the ordinary wage (s.15(5)) |
| Women's hours | 7:00 am to 7:30 pm; later only with written consent plus safety and transport (s.16) |
| Sick leave | 9 days a calendar year; lapses if unused (s.20(2)) |
| Casual leave | 6 days a calendar year; lapses if unused (s.20(2)) |
| Earned leave | 15 days in the next year after 240+ days worked in a calendar year (s.20(3)) |
| EL accumulation | Up to 45 days; EL above 45 can be encashed if leave is refused (s.20(4)–(5)) |
| Paid holidays | 9 a year: Republic Day, Ambedkar Jayanti, May Day, Independence Day, Ganesh Chaturthi, Gandhi Jayanti, Diwali, Goa Liberation Day, Christmas (s.20(6)) |
| Holidays inside leave | Leave excludes all holidays during it or at either end (s.20(8)) |

**What this means for the Panaji head office.** Mon–Fri at 8+ hours a day stays within 48 hours a week. Its holiday calendar must include the 9 named holidays. Staff earning over ₹24,000 or in managerial roles are outside the Act, so Konkan's single policy is more generous than needed for some staff and, on SL, possibly less generous for others (open questions).

**Why the Verna plant follows the OSH Code instead.** A food-processing plant is a factory, which the Goa Act excludes. Factories were governed by the Factories Act, 1948, now replaced by the OSH Code since 21 November 2025 (factoHR). The Code caps work at 8 hours a day (s.25) and 48 a week (Central Rules 2026, rule 64) (Taxclue). It allows no more than 6 working days a week, and gives annual leave of 1 day per 20 days worked after 180 days, carried forward up to 30 days (factoHR). Konkan's Mon–Sat, 8-hour plant schedule is exactly 48 hours.

**Comparison: Maharashtra Shops Act, 2017.** It applies at 10+ workers (Goa: 20+) and caps work at 9 hours a day (Goa: 10), both at 48 a week (Mondaq). It gives 8 days of CL credited quarterly, EL at 1 day per 20 worked (240-day test, up to 45 days), 8 festival holidays and no sick leave, where Goa gives 9 SL (Lawgist, s.18; Nishith Desai Associates).

### Q6. National vs restricted (optional) vs state holiday

National holidays are fixed for everyone, restricted (optional) holidays are picked by each employee from a list, and state holidays are declared by each state.

| Type | What it means |
|---|---|
| National | Three days observed everywhere: Republic Day (26 Jan), Independence Day (15 Aug), Gandhi Jayanti (2 Oct) (factoHR) |
| Gazetted (closed) | Central government offices close on 17 days in 2026; 14 are compulsory nationwide, the rest chosen per state capital (NoBroker) |
| Restricted (optional) | The office stays open; each central government employee picks 2 days a year for their own festivals (factoHR) |
| State | Declared by each state government, for example Goa Liberation Day (19 Dec) in Goa or Kannada Rajyotsava in Karnataka |

Private employers aren't bound by the central list (Bajaj Finserv); they follow their state's Shops Act or factory law. For Konkan, the Goa Act's 9 paid holidays (Q5) are the minimum for covered head-office staff.

**For the HRMS (A7).** Konkan's holiday calendar is per location and each holiday is tagged National, State or Optional. A state holiday added only for Verna must not appear for Panaji. Optional holidays need a per-employee quota and a way to pick them, which the brief doesn't define yet (open questions).

### Q7. Late arrivals, half days, WFH, missed punches

Most companies convert attendance exceptions into leave deductions or LOP, with approval workflows and a payroll cut-off.

| Situation | Common rule |
|---|---|
| Late arrival | 10–15 minute grace period; every 3 late marks in a month = half day, deducted from CL/EL, then LOP. Leave deductions are used instead of fines because the Code on Wages allows fines only for notified acts, capped at 3% of wages. Some firms use flexible timing around core hours instead |
| Half day | Marked when someone works below a threshold, often under 4–5 hours of a 9-hour day; half-day leave allowed for the first or second half |
| Work from home | Separate attendance status counted as present, not leave; needs manager approval; often capped per month or tied to a hybrid roster |
| Missed punch | Employee raises a regularisation request with a reason for manager approval; often limited to about 3 a month, due before payroll cut-off (around the 25th). Unregularised days become absent or LOP, reversed next month if corrected. Every edit keeps an audit trail |

Konkan's rules for these cases are written as testable rules T3–T8 in Part B below.

### Q8. Interview

The interviewee finds their HR tools simple and accurate; their one dislike is having to enter an OTP every time they log in.

**Interviewee:** A colleague working in Accounts Receivable (AR), not named for privacy · **Date:** 20 October 2026 · **Systems they use:** greytHR and Paylocity

| Question | What they said |

| Most frustrating thing | Nothing major; the system is simple and easy to understand |
| One change you'd make | Stay logged in for a period, instead of needing an OTP at every login on both mobile and web |

**What they dislike.** Repeated OTP prompts on both mobile and web. Every login on either device needs OTP confirmation, even for a quick check, so each one costs two extra steps before they reach any task.

**Other things I noticed** (not complaints, but worth noting):
- Their company splits HR across two systems, so the employee has to remember which one holds what.
- Applying for leave takes 7 steps, and 2 of them are only about getting in.
- Tax proofs travel by email, outside both systems.
- Approval takes 1–2 days, so the employee waits without knowing when it will be decided.

**What this means for Konkan's HRMS**
- **Session length:** keep employees signed in on their own device for a set period, using the auth provider's session settings, and ask for re-verification only for sensitive actions such as HR's Reveal of PAN, Aadhaar or bank details. This balances their complaint against the brief's security rules.
- **Fewer steps:** the dashboard shows check-in and leave balances without any clicks (as the brief requires), and Apply leave should be reachable in one tap from it.
- **One system:** employee master, attendance and leave live in one app, so Konkan staff don't juggle tools the way the interviewee does.
- **Faster approvals:** email the manager on every new request (L6) and show pending requests on the manager's dashboard, so approval doesn't stretch over days.

**Limits.** This is one interview with a broadly satisfied user, so it shows what works more than what fails. Its findings should be weighed alongside the problems Konkan already reports in the brief.

### Sandwich rule (required by page 9)

A sandwich rule counts weekends or holidays that fall between two leave days as leave too (Keka).

For example, at Konkan's head office an employee takes Friday and Monday off. Under a sandwich rule, Saturday and Sunday are counted too, so 4 days are deducted. Without it, only 2 days are deducted. Companies use it to stop people stretching short leave into long breaks.

No central law sets a sandwich rule; it is company policy (hirist). Goa's Shops Act points the other way: leave under it is exclusive of all holidays falling during or at either end of the leave period (Goa Act 23 of 2025, s.20(8)).

Konkan has no sandwich rule. Weekly offs and holidays inside a leave period are never counted, so Friday to Monday at the head office counts 2 days, and at the Verna plant (Saturday is a working day) it counts 3.

## Part B — Konkan Coastal Foods policy, written as rules

Every rule below has a number, a short statement and a worked example; the examples become test cases.
- D = employee data rules, T = attendance rules, L = leave rules.
- Rule L1 is not the same as feature L1 in the brief. Feature codes stay as they are; rule codes always carry the word "rule".
- **[Interpretation]** marks a rule where the brief is silent and I chose an answer. Each one is listed in the open questions until faculty confirm it.
- Unmarked rules come straight from the brief.

### Employee data rules (D1–D7)

Letters are changed to capitals and spaces removed before checking. A failed check shows a clear message and nothing is saved.

| Rule | Field | Format and regex | Valid example | Invalid example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | PAN | 5 letters, 4 digits, 1 letter: `^[A-Z]{5}[0-9]{4}[A-Z]$`. Shown as ABCDE\*\*\*\*F | ABCDE1234F | ABC123 (too short) |
| D2 | Aadhaar | 12 digits: `^[0-9]{12}$`. Stored in full, shown only as XXXX XXXX 1234, including in CSV | 123456781234 | 12345678123 (11 digits) |
| D3 | UAN | 12 digits: `^[0-9]{12}$`. Optional: may be left blank for new joiners | 100123456789 or blank | 10012345678A (letter) |
| D4 | IFSC | 4 letters, then 0, then 6 letters or digits: `^[A-Z]{4}0[A-Z0-9]{6}$` | SBIN0001234 | SBIN1001234 (5th character not 0) |
| D5 | Mobile | 10 digits starting 6–9: `^[6-9][0-9]{9}$` | 9876543210 | 987654321 (9 digits), 5876543210 (starts with 5) |
| D6 | PIN and state | PIN is 6 digits: `^[0-9]{6}$`. State is picked from a dropdown of the 28 states and 8 union territories, never typed | 403001, Goa | 40300 (5 digits) |
| D7 | Minimum age | Date of birth must be on or before the joining date minus 18 years | Joins 1 Nov 2026, born 1 Nov 2008 (exactly 18) | Joins 1 Nov 2026, born 2 Nov 2008 (17) |

Bank account numbers are shown as the last 4 digits only (for example XXXXXX4321). HR can see full values only by clicking Reveal, which is logged.

### Attendance rules (T1–T9)

- **T1. One time zone.** All times are stored and shown in IST (Asia/Kolkata), whatever the server's or laptop's time zone. A punch belongs to its IST date. *Example:* a check-in at 00:30 IST on 5 Nov is 19:00 UTC on 4 Nov, but it is recorded on 5 Nov.
- **T2. Weekly offs by location.** Panaji (head office) works Monday–Friday; Saturday and Sunday are weekly offs. Verna (plant) works Monday–Saturday; Sunday is the weekly off. *Example:* Saturday 7 Nov 2026 is a Weekly Off for a Panaji employee and a working day for a Verna employee.
- **T3. Day status from hours worked.** Hours = check-out minus check-in. 8 hours or more = Present. 4 hours up to under 8 = Half Day. Under 4 hours = Absent. *Example:* 09:30–17:30 (8h 00m) = Present; 09:30–17:29 (7h 59m) = Half Day; 09:30–13:30 (4h 00m) = Half Day; 09:30–13:29 (3h 59m) = Absent.
- **T4. Missing punches.** No check-in on a working day = Absent. **[Interpretation]** A check-in with no check-out is also Absent until it is fixed through regularisation (T8). *Example:* checks in at 09:40, forgets to check out → Absent, until the manager approves a corrected check-out.
- **T5. Late.** A check-in after 10:15:00 AM is Late; exactly 10:15:00 is on time. **[Interpretation]** Late does not apply on a WFH day or a day with approved first-half leave. *Example:* 10:15:00 = on time; 10:15:01 = Late; 10:20 = Late.
- **T6. Late marks become LOP.** The 3rd Late mark in a calendar month adds 0.5 day of LOP. **[Interpretation]** Every further 3 Late marks (6th, 9th) add another 0.5 day. The count resets on the 1st of each month. *Example:* Late on 3, 10 and 17 Nov → 0.5 day LOP on 17 Nov. A Late on 2 Dec starts a new count of 1.
- **T7. Work from home.** An employee marks a working day as WFH. It counts as Present, with no hours check, and is counted separately as a WFH day in the month-end CSV. **[Interpretation]** WFH can't be marked on a weekly off, holiday or leave day. *Example:* marks Tuesday as WFH and logs no punches → Present (WFH).
- **T8. Regularisation.** For a missed or wrong punch, the employee requests corrected times with a reason. Attendance does not change until the manager approves; then the day is recalculated with T3. HR can correct a record directly, but must give a reason, which is logged. *Example:* forgot to check out on 4 Nov; requests 09:30–18:00 → still Absent while pending → Present once approved.
- **T9. Holidays and which status wins.** Holidays are set per location and tagged National, State or Optional. A holiday added for one location never appears for the other. When several things apply to a day, the status is decided in this order: Holiday or Weekly Off → On Leave → WFH → punches (T3). *Example:* a State holiday added only for Verna shows as Holiday at Verna and as a normal working day at Panaji. An employee on approved leave who also punches in is On Leave.

### Leave rules (L1–L14)

Dates in examples are from 2026 unless stated, and assume no holidays unless one is named.

- **L1. Leave year.** The leave year runs 1 January to 31 December. *Example:* leave taken on 31 Dec 2026 comes from the 2026 balance; leave on 1 Jan 2027 comes from 2027.
- **L2. Casual Leave (CL).** 8 days, credited on 1 January. Half day allowed. Unused CL lapses on 31 December. *Example:* 3 CL left on 31 Dec → 0 CL carried over; 8 new CL on 1 Jan.
- **L3. Sick Leave (SL).** 7 days, credited on 1 January. Half day allowed. Unused SL lapses on 31 December. *Example:* 7 SL unused on 31 Dec → 0 carried over; 7 new SL on 1 Jan.
- **L4. CL and SL for joiners.** **[Interpretation]** Credit = yearly quota × months left in the year ÷ 12. The joining month counts if the employee joins on or before the 15th. The result is rounded down to the nearest half day. *Example:* joins 1 July (6 months) → 4 CL and 3.5 SL. Joins 15 June (7 months) → 4.67 → 4.5 CL; 4.08 → 4 SL. Joins 16 June (6 months) → 4 CL and 3.5 SL.
- **L5. Earned Leave (EL) accrual.** 1.25 days for each completed month of service (15 a year). No half day. **[Interpretation]** A completed month is a full calendar month; EL is credited on its last day. The joining month counts only if the employee joined on the 1st. *Example:* joins 1 July → first 1.25 EL on 31 July, 7.5 EL by 31 Dec. Joins 15 June → June doesn't count, first 1.25 EL on 31 July, 7.5 EL by 31 Dec. Employed all year → 15 EL by 31 Dec.
- **L6. EL carry-forward.** Up to 30 days of EL carry into the next year. **[Interpretation]** Any excess is flagged in the December summary for the payroll vendor to encash, as the OSH Code requires for plant workers. *Example:* 36 EL on 31 Dec → opening EL of 30 on 1 Jan, and 6 days flagged for encashment.
- **L7. Maternity Leave.** As the law sets (Code on Social Security, s.60): 26 weeks if the employee has fewer than two surviving children (up to 8 before delivery); 12 weeks if two or more (up to 6 before); 12 weeks from handover for adoption or a commissioning mother. She must have worked at least 80 days in the 12 months before the expected delivery. No half day. **[Interpretation]** Counted in calendar days, not working days. *Example:* first child, leave starting 1 Dec 2026 → 182 calendar days, ending 31 May 2027.
- **L8. Paternity Leave.** 5 working days, on request, from the brief's company policy (not the law). No half day. *Example:* Panaji employee starts Thursday 12 Nov → Thu, Fri, Mon, Tue, Wed → back on Thursday 19 Nov.
- **L9. Loss of Pay (LOP).** Unlimited, half day allowed. No balance is kept. *Example:* an employee with no CL left takes 2 days of LOP; both are listed as LOP in the month-end CSV.
- **L10. Counting leave days.** Only working days at the employee's location count (T2). Holidays at that location never count. There is no sandwich rule. A half day counts as 0.5, and is allowed only for CL, SL and LOP. *Example:* Fri 13 Nov to Mon 16 Nov → 2 days at Panaji (Fri, Mon), 3 days at Verna (Fri, Sat, Mon). Wed 23 to Mon 28 Dec, with Christmas on Friday 25 Dec → 3 days at Panaji (Wed, Thu, Mon), 4 at Verna (Wed, Thu, Sat, Mon).
- **L11. Not enough balance.** An employee can't apply for more days than they have available. The app offers to turn the extra days into LOP. *Example:* 1 CL available, applies for 3 days of CL → app offers 1 CL + 2 LOP.
- **L12. Medical certificate.** SL of more than 2 consecutive days needs a certificate upload before the request can be submitted. **[Interpretation]** Days are counted as leave days under L10. *Example:* SL Mon–Wed (3 days) → certificate required. SL Fri + Mon at Panaji (2 days) → not required.
- **L13. Balance terms.** For each leave type:
  - **Opening** = days carried over from last year on 1 January. Only EL has an opening balance (up to 30); CL and SL always start at 0.
  - **Accrued** = days credited this year: CL and SL on 1 January (or on joining, L4); EL at 1.25 per completed month so far (L5).
  - **Used** = days in approved requests, past or future.
  - **Pending** = days in requests that are submitted but not yet decided.
  - **Available** = opening + accrued − used − pending. It is calculated each time, never stored.

  Used changes only when a request is approved. Pending holds days back so they can't be requested twice, but it is not a deduction. *Example:* EL on 1 Nov: opening 10, accrued 12.5, used 5, pending 2 → available 15.5. If approved: used 7, pending 0, available 15.5. If rejected: pending 0, available 17.5.
- **L14. Applying, approving and cancelling.**
  - Leave can't start more than 30 days before today.
  - The employee's reporting manager approves or rejects with a comment. An HR Admin can approve or reject anyone's leave. Nobody approves their own.
  - **[Interpretation]** Someone with no manager (such as the managing director) is approved by an HR Admin. The HR Admin's own leave goes to their reporting manager, so the seed data must give the HR Admin a manager.
  - An employee can cancel a pending request, or an approved one that hasn't started. The days come back to the balance.

  *Example:* on 24 Oct, the earliest start date allowed is 24 Sep. Approved leave for 10–12 Nov is cancelled on 5 Nov → used drops by 3. On 11 Nov the same leave can no longer be cancelled.
